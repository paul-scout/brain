# FHP Mastery

## Why High Price Stocks Are Better

### Position Sizing Mathematics

- Smaller percentage stops allow **larger position sizes risk-adjusted**
- 2% stop = 50% of account can be deployed
- 10% stop (sugar babies) = only 10% of account
- Same dollar risk, dramatically different growth trajectory

### Institutional Participation

- Institutions quietly trade high price stocks
- More reliable signals and **longer duration moves**
- Less noise than retail-favorite low price stocks
- Signals more sustainable

### Less Crowded = Better Risk/Reward

- Retail traders obsess over $1-$5 stocks (lottery tickets)
- Crowded trades = worse risk/reward
- High price stocks are **less crowded uncrowded opportunities**
- Better asymmetry for patient traders

### Lifestyle Fit

- High price stocks move slowly over **5-10 trading days**
- **No babysitting required** during the day
- Ideal for working people with full-time jobs
- Setups in morning, check end of day — that's it

## The Psychology Barrier

- The **psychological barrier to high price stocks is the real obstacle**, not capital
- Retail traders email asking for low price stock recommendations
- They cannot overcome their belief systems mentally
- "The motion in the ocean matters more than account size"

## Deep Dive Over Consumption

- Studying **stocks that made $20+ moves** reveals how breakouts started
- Deep dive on specific setups teaches more than reading multiple books
- Content consumption ≠ skill development
- Practical study > passive learning

## The Sugar Babies Problem

- Everyone wants sugar babies ($3-$15 stocks)
- FHP stocks are "like people nobody wants to date"
- But: less competition = better entry points
- Less crowded = better risk/reward

## Same Rules Apply

- **Same SOS and momentum burst rules** apply to FHP
- The stock selection criteria differs (low % breakouts)
- Entry timing and management rules are identical
- This is not a different strategy — it's the same strategy on different stocks

## Prioritization Rule

- When you have **multiple setups**, always prioritize the **highest priced stock**
- Same dollar risk = larger potential reward
- Size compound over time

## Key Insights Summary

1. Crowded trades attract retail; uncrowded FHP = superior risk/reward
2. Small thinking creates small accounts
3. Position sizing math favors high price stocks
4. Deep dive study > consuming content
5. Working traders benefit most from FHP (minimal babysitting)
6. Psychological barrier is the real obstacle, not capital
7. Risk/reward asymmetry comes from low % breakouts + significant dollar moves
